Signals in the Evidence

“I opened the stage times on the festival website. Why could I see my name and the ticket I bought?” A visitor has sent this message to the Clydebeat organiser.

How did visitors’ personal details end up on the public noticeboard?

Festival Updates is the public noticeboard on the Clydebeat website. Anyone can read it without signing in. Visitors give their names and booking details to the gate team so staff can check their tickets. If those details appear here, strangers can see who booked tickets and what they bought.

Open the post in step 1 and use the clues to explain how that happened.

Teacher facilitation notes

Allow around 10–15 minutes for this demo. Pupils explain how a public post exposed visitors’ personal details, support their finding with the attachment and its audience, then recommend a response. Each step shows the records pupils need. Pupils can retry a step and review earlier steps. For discussion, ask why a shared account makes it difficult to identify the person who used it. CyberScot saves the completed demo record online with its practice code, answers and attempt counts. Use fictional codes for demonstrations. The result can be reopened by anyone who has its private link.
